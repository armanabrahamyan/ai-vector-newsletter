Currents

3 items

All three items today measure something practitioners assumed was already solved: prompt cost, session memory, and fine-tuning fidelity. The aggregate direction is that agent pipelines carry hidden degradation at scale, and current benchmarks are not yet reliable enough to catch it.

Shopify compresses agent system prompts to cut latency by 38%

System prompt cost was paid in full at every inference call; gisting makes it a one-time training expense.

Shopify's gisting trains a small set of learned tokens to replace a long system prompt, leaving model weights untouched. Their Sidekick agent's 6,000-token prompt collapsed to 1,500 tokens; end-to-end latency fell from 6.8 to 4.2 seconds, freeing GPU capacity. Shopify's own numbers, but the mechanism is sound: factor it into your next inference-cost architecture decision.

Banking AI assistants forget customer history as conversations pile up

Banking assistant accuracy now has a measured floor: recall falls by a fifth over 300 sessions.

An arXiv preprint tests eleven language models on 6,000 synthetic Korean banking dialogues, tracking 34 financial-state paths across up to 300 sessions. Event recall falls from 0.591 at 15 sessions to 0.445 at 300; models routinely treat superseded data as current. The benchmark and its 6,000-dialogue harness are publicly available for teams building longitudinal assistants.

Fine-tuning benchmarks measure output format, not model capability

Fine-tuning quality scores were measuring answer format; the skill itself stayed hidden.

An arXiv preprint tested 12 tasks across three model families and found that answer format, not content, drives standard fine-tuning quality metrics. One skill raised accuracy by over 40 points under its training format yet became nearly invisible under others. The preprint and its benchmark data are public on arXiv, covering all three model families and all 12 tasks.
