Safety guardrail scores measure training labels, not actual detection ability

Safety benchmark scores previously signalled detection strength; they measure label-policy alignment instead.

An arXiv preprint trained ten guardrail classifiers on the same 53,000 prompts under two labeling policies and found benchmark scores track policy agreement (rho +0.74) rather than detection capability (rho +0.34). Changing 0.03% of parameters shifted one benchmark across its full range. When a vendor presents a safety score, do you know which labeling definition it was graded against?

Cheap eval classifiers underperform unless you tune their decision threshold

Eval pipelines defaulting to a 0.50 threshold routinely miss failures a tuned threshold catches.

Hamel Husain's guide benchmarks five decision models (Perplexity Decider, Liquid d1, Cloudflare Clef, OpenAI Decisions, and TypeSafe Jev) against 100 labelled agent traces. The 0.50 default threshold consistently underperforms; cross-validated tuning lifted F1 scores on every model tested. Before relying on any decision model in your eval pipeline, run threshold tuning on your own labelled traces first.
